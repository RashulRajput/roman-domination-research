# Novelty and literature audit

**Update (2026-10-26):** a new targeted audit of exact tree extremality and
open-irredundance appears in
[EXTREMAL_CONJECTURE_AUDIT.md](EXTREMAL_CONJECTURE_AUDIT.md); the exhaustive
census now reaches order 20. The pointwise formulas remain conjectures.

**Search cutoff:** 2026-10-26. This is a targeted novelty audit, not a proof that no unpublished, non-indexed, or differently worded result exists. “No exact hit” below is evidence about the searches listed here, not a novelty proof.

## Findings by proposed contribution

| Proposed contribution | Nearest prior result and exact overlap | Difference in the present result | Novelty confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bijection between minimal RDFs and qualifying sets of 2-vertices; “strong irredundance” | Abu-Khzam, Fernau, and Mann prove that a minimal RDF is uniquely determined by (V_2), and their 2024 paper calls the associated condition “strong irredundance.” The same set property was already called **open irredundance**: every selected vertex has an external private neighbor. See [Algorithmica 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00453-024-01211-w), and the prior [open-irredundance paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2007.10.002). | No new correspondence or irredundance notion is claimed here. The useful formulation for trees is (m(G)=\#\{S\subseteq V: \forall s\in S,\exists u\in N(s),\ N(u)\cap S=\{s\}\}\), including (S=\emptyset). | **None** for the correspondence or terminology. |
| Exact formula (m(T_k)=3^k+2^k-1), where (T_k) has one center and (k) arms of length two | The same numerical expression occurs in Theorem 3.4 of [Roman Census](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.MFCS.2023.6), as a lower-bound count for a different (3t)-vertex split/cobipartite construction. | That paper does not give this exact formula for the (2k+1)-vertex subdivided-star tree. The tree proof here splits according to whether the center is in (V_2). A direct exact-tree-family search found no matching publication, but this does not establish priority. | **Low to moderate.** The tree-specific identity may be new; the expression and the counting framework are not. |
| (T_5) beats the path: (m(T_5)=274>m(P_{11})=273) | [Roman Census](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.MFCS.2023.6) gives recurrences/counting bounds for paths and a forest upper bound. It does not assert that paths maximize (m(T)) over connected trees. | This is an exact counterexample to the proposed path-extremality statement. It also disproves the broader connected-chordal path-extremality claim, since trees are chordal. Both counts are verified by three independent exact counters in the attached census. | **Moderate as a counterexample; priority unresolved.** No exact statement or counterexample was located in the targeted search. |
| Infinite connected-tree lower bound with root rate $\sqrt3$, and hence $\limsup C(n)^{1/n}=\sqrt3$ | Roman Census proves $m(F)\le(\sqrt3)^{|V(F)|}$ for every forest and proves sharpness using disjoint copies of $P_2$. This is a **disconnected** construction. Its path upper bound is $m(P_n)=O^*(1.6852^n)$. | The connected trees $T_k$ have order $2k+1$ and $m(T_k)=3^k+2^k-1$, so their per-vertex rate tends to $\sqrt3$. The forest theorem supplies the matching upper bound. Thus the connected-tree **limsup** follows; this does not establish a limit for every order or identify $C(n)$ pointwise. No already-published connected-tree construction with this rate was found in the sources and searches below. | **Moderate-low.** It is not supplied by the published disconnected $P_2$-union example. The result is a short corollary once the $T_k$ formula is known; priority remains unresolved. |
| (C(n)=\max_{|V(T)|=n}m(T)) and all maximizers through (n=20) | Roman Census treats path counts and the forest-wide bound, not a census of extremal tree isomorphism classes. Rote’s tree extremal theorem counts **minimal dominating sets**, a different object: [SODA 2019 paper/manuscript](https://page.mi.fu-berlin.de/rote/Papers/pdf/Minimal%2Bdominating%2Bsets%2Bin%2Ba%2Btree%3A%2Bcounting%2C%2Band%2Benumeration%2C%2Band%2Bextremal%2Bresults.pdf). | The finite values and all extremal classes through order 20 are recorded in `COUNTEREXAMPLE_SEARCH.md` and the machine-readable census. Every individual count at orders 13–20 agrees between two rooted-DP implementations; a subset counter additionally checked all extremizers and the path/T_k counts. | **Unknown/low.** Exhaustive finite data alone is not a general extremal theorem. |
| Rooted state recurrence for exact tree counts | The MRDF-to-(V_2) characterization is prior; generic tree dynamic programming is standard. Rote gives a different DP for minimal dominating sets. | A recurrence for the open-irredundant/(V_2) condition is specified and proved in `STRUCTURAL_PROOF_ATTEMPTS.md`; it tracks the parent selection bit, selected-child count (capped at two), and whether the root still needs a parent witness. Array and bounded-memo implementations agree with the audited recurrence on all 987 trees through order 12; they also agree on all stored counts for orders 13–20. | **Low / unassessed.** It is a correct useful recurrence; no claim of algorithmic novelty is made. |

The published path bound in Roman Census, (m(P_n)=O^*(1.6852^n)), combined with the exact formula gives (m(T_k)>m(P_{2k+1})) for all sufficiently large (k), since (sqrt3>1.6852). This is a proved consequence of the family formula and the published path estimate, not an additional census extrapolation. The exact order-11 instance is separately verified in the three-counter census.

## Search record

In the displayed set-counting characterization, the private witness must
also satisfy \(u\notin S\). This exclusion is part of “external private
neighbor” and is required for the corresponding RDF vertex to be zero.

## Literature actually reviewed

The full 15-page MFCS 2023 Roman Census paper and its full arXiv version were reviewed, including Theorem 3.4, the path recurrences, Theorem 7.1, the chordal-graph discussion, conclusion, and references. The full 2024 Algorithmica article was reviewed for the minimality characterization, (V_2)-bijection, lower-bound constructions, and strong-irredundance discussion. The full Bród paper and Rote manuscript were checked because they are the directly relevant cited tree/path counting results. The open-irredundance sources and the three later related papers above were also checked.

This audit follows the citations relevant to counting, trees, paths, and private-neighbor terminology; it does not claim to have read every unrelated Roman-domination optimization paper in the Roman Census bibliography.
